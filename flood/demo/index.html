    Does the gap repeat? Blind test of the score

    Counties scored on 1978–2014 claims only, then checked on 2015–2024. “Ran high” means losses at least 1.5× the model, relative to the US norm for that decade.

    How a call is made. Expected loss = FEMA National Risk Index modeled flood loss to buildings (inland + coastal, v1.20, Dec 2025) × the share of homes with NFIP cover (Aug 2026). Actual = NFIP building claims paid per year, 1978–2025, in 2024 dollars. Each county is compared with the US-wide ratio for the same years, with its single worst year left out so one storm doesn't decide the call. The result is shrunk toward 1 when the county has few claims (full weight at 1,082 paid claims). Pause: 3× or more. Re-price: 1.5× or more. Grow: 0.8× or less, with under 10% of homes insured. Hold: everything else.

    How the lists are ranked. Grow: modeled loss on homes without NFIP cover. Only affordable counties whose last ten years agree are included, at most 2 per state so the list never sits in one storm path. Re-price and Pause: strength of evidence. Only counties with 300+ paid claims whose last ten years agree are included.

    Limits. Private flood cover isn't public by county, so “homes without NFIP cover” is an upper bound on headroom, especially in Florida and Texas. Today's insured share stands in for past years. The model is FEMA's public one, not the insurer's own. Affordability uses Census ACS 2019–2023 median household income and home value; “modeled loss per home” spreads loss to all buildings over homes, so it overstates the cost per home.

    Sources: OpenFEMA (NFIP claims v3, residential penetration rates, disaster declarations) · FEMA National Risk Index counties · Census ACS summary files · BLS CPI-U.
